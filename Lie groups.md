A lie group is a [[group]] that is also a [[Manifold]]
An example of such a group would be the set of rotations in 3d. Its a manifold because every rotation can be described as a point (eg the axis of rotation multiplied by the angle gives a unique point to each rotation inside a sphere of radius $\pi$)
Lie groups are often defined by some constraint on the transformation the elements of the group represent over a base object (ie conserving length of vectors if the group is rotations R^TR = I)
Every lie group has a corresponding [[Lie algebra]] which is the tangent vector space to the manifold represented by the lie group, at the point which represents the identity element. Examples of lie algebras are [[Complex numbers]] [[Quaternions]] [[Spinors]] and the rotation matrix basis.
They dont necesarily live in the manifold however every point in the manifold can be constructed by exponentiating a vector in the lie algebra for example [[Euler's formula]]. The vector which is exponentiated is called the generator, because it generates a curve on the manifold given by $e^{tV}$ for t in some range. This is why we have phrases like *momentum is the generator of displacement* in [[quantum mechanics]]. The use of generator in a group is eerily similar to how generators are exponentiated in [[cyclic groups]].
